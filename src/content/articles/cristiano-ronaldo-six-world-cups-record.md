Cristiano Ronaldo appeared at six FIFA World Cups. No player in the men's international game has done more. He scored at all of them. He never won one.

That tension, the relentless pursuit of the one prize that never came, is the story inside the story of Ronaldo's international career. He won a European Championship. He won Nations League titles. He rewrote every individual record the game had to offer. And across 20 years of trying, across six tournaments on four continents, the World Cup remained exactly where it started: out of reach.

This is the story of Ronaldo and the World Cup, six chapters that together tell you everything about what he was as a player and what it cost him to be it.

## 2006: The Debut (Germany)

Ronaldo was 21 at his first World Cup, already a Manchester United player, already showing the shape of what he was going to become. Portugal reached the semi-finals under Luiz Felipe Scolari, their best World Cup finish since Eusebio's generation in 1966.

They lost to France, 1-0. Ronaldo scored at the tournament, contributed to the run, and left with the knowledge that Portugal could compete at the highest level. It was an encouraging debut. It set the bar.

The controversy of that tournament, the wink after Wayne Rooney's red card in the quarter-final, followed Ronaldo back to Manchester United and shaped how the football world perceived him for years. It did not diminish what he produced on the pitch.

## 2010: The Exit (South Africa)

South Africa 2010 was harder. Portugal got past the group stage and beat North Korea but were eliminated by eventual winners Spain in the round of 16, 1-0, through a David Villa goal. Ronaldo scored once at the tournament.

It was the kind of exit that happens to good teams when they face a great one. Spain went on to win it. Portugal went home. Ronaldo was 25 and already one of the best players in the world. The gap between his individual level and what his country could achieve as a team was becoming visible.

## 2014: Group Stage Exit (Brazil)

Brazil 2014 was the worst of the six. Portugal finished third in their group, failing to advance past the group stage. Ronaldo was carrying a physical problem through the tournament and was clearly not at his best. Portugal beat Ghana but drew with the United States and lost to Germany 4-0.

He was 29. The window for World Cup success was not closing, but it was not open as wide as it had been. The tournament that should have been a platform felt, by the end, like a limitation.

## 2018: The Last 16 (Russia)

Russia 2018 gave the world one of Ronaldo's greatest individual World Cup moments: his hat-trick against Spain in the group stage, the third goal a free-kick in the 88th minute that levelled the match at 3-3. It is one of the best individual performances in World Cup history.

Portugal were eliminated by Uruguay in the last 16. Edinson Cavani scored twice. Ronaldo, at 33, had given the tournament one brilliant night and been unable to give it more.

The hat-trick against Spain will be replayed forever. The elimination will be forgotten more quickly. That is the nature of these things.

## 2022: Quarter-Finals (Qatar)

Qatar 2022 was the closest he came to real World Cup progress. Portugal beat Switzerland in the round of 16, with Ronaldo scoring from the penalty spot in what turned out to be his last World Cup goal. They were eliminated by Morocco in the quarter-finals, one of the greatest upsets in World Cup history, as an African nation reached the semi-finals for the first time.

Ronaldo was 37. Morocco won 1-0. He cried on the pitch after the final whistle.

The images of him leaving the stadium that night are among the most affecting of his career. A player who had given 20 years to this pursuit, reduced to tears on a pitch in Qatar, the dream ending without warning to a team that Portugal had not expected to lose to.

## 2026: The Last Try (North America)

His sixth and final World Cup was held across the United States, Canada and Mexico. Ronaldo was 41, and the gap between what he had been and what he was now was visible in a way it had not been at previous tournaments.

Portugal did not win it. The tournament was difficult, with criticism of Ronaldo for starting up front as his ability to influence games physically diminished. The relationship between him and manager Jorge Jesus was under strain. When it was over, he announced his retirement from international football.

The World Cup, the only major prize that eluded him in a career that collected almost everything else, stays that way. Six attempts across 20 years. No winner's medal.

## What Six World Cups Means

The record of six World Cup appearances is one that will not be broken quickly, if it is broken at all. Modern football makes it harder to maintain an international career across 20 years without injury, without being replaced, without a manager deciding the future belongs to someone else.

Ronaldo was never replaced. He was at every World Cup because he was always, until the very end, good enough to be there. That in itself is a remarkable achievement, separate from the medals.

He never won the World Cup. He scored at all six of them, he reached the semi-finals at the first one, the quarter-finals in his later years, and he gave the tournament some of its greatest individual moments along the way.

It was not everything he wanted. It was more than almost any player in history has managed to give.
