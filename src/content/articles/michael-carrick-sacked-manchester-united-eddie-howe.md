Manchester United are heading into the November international break with a decision to make and very little time to make it comfortably. Reports published on 8 and 9 November indicate the club has effectively made up its mind: Michael Carrick will be sacked, the question is when it is announced and who comes in.

## The 4-4 That Was the Last Straw

The trigger was Sunday's 4-4 draw with Aston Villa at Old Trafford. United came from behind to level and even looked like they might take three points at one stage, but the final scoreline of four goals conceded at home cemented a reality the numbers had been building toward for weeks.

United have conceded more goals than any club in Europe's top five leagues this season. They are 18th in the Premier League table. They have won once in nine games across all competitions. The 4-4 draw against Villa was not a turning point, it was a confirmation.

Reports indicate the club hierarchy, including Sir Jim Ratcliffe, Jason Wilcox, Omar Berrada and Christopher Vivell, will convene during the break to formalise the decision. The sacking is expected before the next league fixture, a trip to Liverpool.

## The Case for Keeping Him Is Gone

A month ago, the case for patience had some basis. New signings needed time to build chemistry. The squad had been extensively rebuilt over the summer. The £8.8m sacking cost was a real consideration for a club managing its finances carefully.

Three more losses and the 4-4 have stripped away every version of that argument. A team that concedes four goals at home to Aston Villa on a Sunday afternoon in November has stopped improving. The patience the board extended after the Bournemouth defeat has been used up.

Gary Neville said he was surprised United kept Carrick after Bournemouth. The 4-4 against Villa produced no such surprise. The reporting from every credible source is pointing in one direction.

## Eddie Howe: The Leading Candidate

Eddie Howe's name has emerged as the frontrunner. The former Bournemouth and Newcastle manager brings Championship promotion experience, Premier League success at mid-table level, and a reputation for developing players and implementing a coherent attacking philosophy.

His availability, combined with his profile as someone who can restore confidence in a struggling squad while also producing good football, makes him a logical choice for a club that needs immediate results but cannot afford to hire someone only for the short term.

Julian Nagelsmann has also been mentioned, though the German coach's last tenure at Bayern Munich ended under difficult circumstances and his appointment at a struggling club in a new league would represent a different challenge from his previous roles.

## What Happens Next

The international break ends with United needing to travel to Anfield. Whoever is in charge for that fixture, whether it is an interim or a new permanent appointment, faces an immediate test against a Liverpool side that just beat Crystal Palace 3-1.

United's season is at a crossroads that one manager change alone cannot resolve. The squad has quality, the results suggest something structural is wrong, and the club is 18th in a league where the gap to safety is currently one point. Time is running short.
