Europe's premier club competition roared back to life with one of the most extraordinary opening matchdays in recent memory. From Paris to Madrid, Munich to Liverpool, the continent's elite clubs delivered a collective statement of intent that left supporters breathless and set the tone for what promises to be a fascinating campaign ahead.

## PSG 6-1 Slovan Bratislava: Luis Enrique's Men Announce Themselves

Paris Saint-Germain wasted no time reminding Europe of their ambitions. At the Parc des Princes, the French giants dismantled Slovakian champions Slovan Bratislava with a ruthlessness that bordered on clinical. Six goals with barely a reply told the story of a contest that was effectively over long before the final whistle. The scoreline flattered Slovan if anything, and it announced PSG as serious contenders for the crown they have long coveted. Luis Enrique's system pressed from the front with relentless energy, and Slovan found no way to cope with the pace and movement on display throughout.

## Barcelona 5-1 Feyenoord: The Flick Machine Rolls On

If PSG's result was emphatic, Barcelona's performance under Hansi Flick was arguably even more impressive in terms of the football on display. Feyenoord, a team with genuine European pedigree, were swept aside in a manner that underlined just how dominant this Barcelona side has become in the early weeks of the season. Ferran Torres was the star of the show, completing a hat-trick that showcased his movement, composure, and clinical finishing in equal measure. The Spanish international has emerged as one of the most lethal forwards in Europe under Flick's guidance, and his display in this contest will have sent a shiver down the spines of defenders across the continent.

The 5-1 scoreline represents a continuation of Barcelona's devastating form in La Liga, where they sit top of the table unbeaten after seven games. Lamine Yamal dazzled down the right flank as ever, while the collective pressing and combination play drew comparisons to some of the great Barcelona sides of the past. Feyenoord, to their credit, never stopped competing, but the gulf in quality was simply too vast to bridge on the night.

## Bayern Munich 5-0 Bodo/Glimt: Ruthless German Efficiency

From Catalonia to Bavaria, the story was the same. Bayern Munich made light work of Norwegian champions Bodo/Glimt, posting a comprehensive 5-0 victory that underlined their status as perennial contenders. Demirovic stole the headlines with a remarkable hat-trick that displayed the striker's instinct in front of goal, capping off a display of incisive forward play that will have the Bundesliga club buzzing heading into the next round of fixtures. Bodo/Glimt, whose attacking football has earned them admirers across Europe in recent years, were simply overwhelmed by the occasion and the quality arrayed against them. Bayern's press was relentless, their transitions sharp, and their finishing lethal.

## Real Madrid 2-1 Inter Milan: Champions Tested But Triumphant

The clash between Real Madrid and Inter Milan at the Santiago Bernabeu was, predictably, the evening's most gripping contest. Where other matches became processions, this was a proper European encounter filled with tension, quality, and a genuinely competitive edge. Inter, one of the few sides with the organisation and talent to trouble the reigning champions, made Real work hard for every moment of the contest.

Madrid ultimately edged it 2-1, but the Italian giants gave a creditable account of themselves and will travel back to Milan knowing they pushed the Merengue closer than most. For Real Madrid, who have shown a somewhat inconsistent edge domestically this term sitting fourth in La Liga on fifteen points, the result represents exactly the sort of gritty European win that has defined their history in this competition.

## Liverpool 2-1 Atletico Madrid: Iraola's Men Deliver a Statement

Perhaps the most significant result in terms of the wider narrative of the evening was Liverpool's 2-1 victory over Atletico Madrid. Andoni Iraola's team, navigating a significant period of transition following the departure of Mohamed Salah and the summer arrivals of Florian Wirtz, Alexander Isak, and Bradley Barcola, produced a display that suggested the rebuilding process is further along than some had anticipated.

Michael Olise contributed to the night's action, further confirming his status as a player on a steep upward trajectory. Liverpool's win against Diego Simeone's famously combative Atletico side was earned through pressing intensity and a tactical discipline that bore the clear imprint of Iraola's methods. For supporters who feared the transition away from the Slot era might be painful, this result offered considerable reassurance.

## The Bigger Picture

Taken as a whole, Matchday 1 of the 2026-27 Champions League delivered exactly what the competition promises at its best: drama, goals, and moments that remind you why club football at the European level occupies a unique place in the sporting imagination. The total goals scored across these fixtures alone amounted to a staggering return, with PSG, Barcelona, and Bayern each posting five or more.

As the group stages begin to take shape, several teams have already announced themselves as forces to be reckoned with. Barcelona's form feels almost frightening given their domestic dominance. PSG will fancy their chances with their depth and ambition. Bayern's clinical edge remains as sharp as ever. And Liverpool, refreshed and reinvented, may yet be the wildcard that disrupts the established order.

One matchday in, and already the Champions League has delivered. The rest of the tournament cannot come quickly enough.
