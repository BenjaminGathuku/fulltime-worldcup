West Ham United have sacked Nuno Espirito Santo after a start to the Championship season that the club's board deemed unacceptable. The Portuguese manager, who took the job after West Ham's relegation from the Premier League, was dismissed with immediate effect following consecutive home defeats that left the club eighth in the second-tier table.

## What Went Wrong

Nuno arrived at West Ham carrying the expectation that a club of their resources, playing in the Championship, would be among the automatic promotion candidates. The reality after thirteen rounds has been considerably more complicated.

Back-to-back home defeats to Southampton and West Bromwich Albion were the results that ended his tenure. Those results, against two of the division's other well-resourced clubs, left West Ham in eighth position, one place outside the play-off places. For a club that had invested in the squad expecting to challenge at the top of the table, eighth after thirteen games was not where the board wanted to be.

The two results together constituted a final verdict. The board decided that the trajectory was not improving and that the club needed different leadership to meet the standard they had set for themselves going into the season.

## Nuno's Short Tenure

Nuno Espirito Santo's West Ham tenure was brief. He had previously managed Sporting CP, Porto, Valencia, Wolves (where he won promotion to the Premier League), Spurs and Al Ittihad. His record at Championship level was limited, and the demands of a club expecting immediate promotion in a highly competitive second division proved difficult to navigate.

His assistant coach Rui Pedro Silva and goalkeeping coach Rui Barbosa also left the club with him. The full coaching staff departure gives whoever comes next a clean slate to build their own setup.

In the short term, Mark Noble, Steve Potts and Gerard Prenderville will oversee first-team preparations, starting with the home match against Cardiff City.

## Who Replaces Him

Scott Parker has been identified as the frontrunner for the job. Parker, who won promotion from the Championship with Bournemouth in 2022, brings direct experience of what it takes to get out of the second division with a club that has promotion expectations. His availability and his track record in this division make him a logical choice.

Rob Edwards is also under consideration. Edwards has built a reputation for developing teams with an attractive style of play and has Championship management experience.

The search for a permanent appointment is underway. West Ham's hierarchy are aware that the window for making the automatic promotion places remains open, but the gap between eighth and the top two is already significant enough that a fast start under the new manager will be required.

## A Warning for the Others

West Ham's situation is a reminder that Championship football is unforgiving regardless of the resources a club brings down from the Premier League. The division has depth, the fixtures are relentless, and the gap between the top six and the rest can open up quickly if a club stumbles at the start.

They are still within reach of the play-off places after thirteen games. The window is not closed. But the margin for continued error is narrow, and whoever takes charge has very little time to impose their methods before the season's shape becomes fixed.
