The 2026-27 Premier League season arrived a week later than usual. FIFA's expanded World Cup calendar pushed everything back, and clubs returned from the summer with players in various states of exhaustion, triumph, and heartbreak. The season that follows a World Cup is always unpredictable. This one has lived up to that.

## The title race: Arsenal and City level

Arsenal and Manchester City have picked up exactly where they left off, which is to say at the top of the table and level on points. Both clubs have won their first four matches. Both have conceded very little. The title race between them, which has defined English football for much of the last decade, has resumed without any of the early-season stumbles that sometimes create breathing room.

Arsenal's summer was defined by retention rather than splash. Mikel Arteta kept his core together and added quietly. The results so far suggest a squad that knows exactly what it is doing. Manchester City's story is more complicated: Enzo Maresca arrived as head coach after several years at Leicester and then Juventus, inheriting a squad that Pep Guardiola built and left at the peak of its powers. Maresca has not changed much. He has not needed to.

Brentford sit third on nine points, unbeaten in five matches, which is the kind of story that sounds implausible in September and obvious in retrospect. Thomas Frank has built a club that punches far above its resources, and the early-season form suggests another top-half finish at minimum.

## The new arrivals making noise

Xabi Alonso took charge at Chelsea in the summer and the transformation has been immediate. Chelsea sit seventh with seven points from five games, which sounds modest but understates how different the club looks under their new manager. The 4-3 win over Brighton in August, the highest-scoring match of the season so far, showed a Chelsea side willing to attack with abandon. Alonso's fingerprints are visible in how the team presses, how it transitions, and how it uses the ball.

Joao Pedro won the Premier League Player of the Month award for August, scoring three goals and contributing two assists in the opening weeks. At 24, the Brazilian forward is playing the best football of his career at Stamford Bridge.

Manchester United under their own new structure produced one of the results of the early season: a 5-2 win over Ipswich Town in which Bruno Fernandes scored a hat-trick. Fernandes, the Portugal captain who was at the centre of the Ronaldo-Jorge Jesus dispute during the international break, arrived back at Old Trafford and immediately reminded everyone why he remains one of the best midfielders in the world.

## The struggles: Spurs, Fulham, and pointless Coventry

At the other end of the table, Tottenham Hotspur have two points from five games and occupy the relegation zone. The club that has spent years promising a return to Champions League football finds itself, at least in these early weeks, fighting a different kind of battle. Four games without a win, questions about the manager, questions about the recruitment. It is early, but the mood around the club is not good.

Coventry City are the story that stops conversations at their tracks. Promoted to the Premier League for the first time in their modern history, they have played four games, lost all four, and have not scored a single goal. Zero points, zero goals, conceded 11. Relegation is not mathematically certain in October, but the numbers are brutal.

Fulham on one point and Coventry on zero make up two of the bottom three. The third changes from week to week.

## Hull City and the surprise package

Hull City sit fifth on eight points. Their manager Sergej Jakirovic won the Premier League Manager of the Month award for August. Hull, promoted via the Championship, have built a side that is difficult to beat, organised in defensive shape, and dangerous on the counter. Whether they can sustain this through winter and the fixture list that comes with it is the question. But for now, they deserve every moment of the spotlight.

## What to watch in October

The international break swallowed two weeks of October, with the Nations League taking centre stage. When clubs return in the second half of the month, the fixtures tighten. Arsenal host Manchester City in what could be the defining early-season statement. Chelsea travel to Liverpool. Tottenham, desperately needing points, face a run of matches they cannot afford to lose.

The season that began late is now fully alive. Both title contenders are perfect. The promoted clubs are scattered across the table from fifth to twentieth. And the managers who arrived in the summer, Alonso at Chelsea, Maresca at City, Iraola at Liverpool, are all still in the process of showing whether their early impressions will hold.

In the Premier League, they usually do not hold for long without being tested. October is where the testing begins.
