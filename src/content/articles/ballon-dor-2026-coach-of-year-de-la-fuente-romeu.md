The Coach of the Year category at the Ballon d'Or ceremony in London produced outcomes that few would have disputed. Luis de la Fuente and Pere Romeu won the men's and women's awards respectively, both recognised for leading their teams to the highest levels of achievement over the reference period.

## Luis de la Fuente: The World Cup Winner

Luis de la Fuente became Spain's World Cup-winning manager on the back of a tournament that confirmed Spain as the dominant force in international football. His win at the 2026 Ballon d'Or Coach of the Year award recognises not just the trophy, but the manner in which Spain played their football across the tournament.

De la Fuente's Spain were a team built on clarity of identity. The pressing game, the technical quality in midfield, and the willingness to trust young players in the biggest moments defined his approach. Lamine Yamal, just 19, was trusted to play the full 120 minutes of the World Cup final. Unai Simon was Spain's unquestioned first choice in goal. The manager's trust in those players, and in the system he had developed, was rewarded with the game's biggest prize.

Spain's journey to the 2026 World Cup title represents De la Fuente's vindication. He took over a national team that had already won the Euros under his tenure, and adding a World Cup to that achievement places him among the most successful international managers of his era. The Ballon d'Or recognition, awarded by 100 journalists who cover the game closely, reflects a consensus that his work with Spain has been the defining coaching achievement of the reference period.

## Pere Romeu: Barcelona's Women's Architect

Pere Romeu's award on the women's side reflects Barcelona's sustained dominance of European women's football. The Spanish club have been the standard-bearers for the women's game for several years, and Romeu's management of a squad containing multiple Ballon d'Or nominees speaks to an organisational and tactical quality that goes beyond simply assembling talent.

Managing a squad of Barcelona's ambition and quality brings its own pressures. Keeping players motivated across domestic and European competition, developing a system that serves the collective while allowing individual talents to flourish, and sustaining the kind of winning culture that produces trophies consistently are all management challenges that Romeu has navigated successfully.

The women's Club of the Year award also went to Barcelona at Monday's ceremony, providing a collective context for Romeu's individual recognition. The club and the manager have been inseparable from the best of women's football this cycle, and both have been recognised accordingly.

## What the Coaching Awards Reflect

Both winners share a common thread: collective success at the highest level. De la Fuente won a World Cup. Romeu won the women's Club of the Year. The Ballon d'Or voting body, in awarding these coaches, has underlined that the award retains its commitment to recognising achievement rather than reputation, the manager whose team actually performed, rather than the manager with the bigger name.
