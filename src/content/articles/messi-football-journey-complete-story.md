In 1998, a boy in Rosario, Argentina was told he would not grow. His body was not producing enough growth hormone, and without medical intervention, he would never reach the height needed to play top-level football. The treatment cost money his family and local clubs could not find.

A scout from Barcelona flew to Argentina and watched the boy play. He went back to Spain and told the club they had to sign this child. The contract was reportedly written on a napkin. Barcelona would pay for the treatment. The boy would move to Spain at 13 and join their academy.

That boy was Lionel Messi. What followed is the most complete career in football history.

## Rosario and the beginning

Jorge Messi and Celia Cuccittini had three children, and their youngest son was born on 24 June 1987 in Rosario, Argentina. From the earliest age, the story was the same: this child was different. He played with older boys and made them look ordinary. His grandmother, Celia, took him to training, drove him to matches, believed in him before anyone else. She died in 2000, when Messi was 12, before she could watch what he became. He has celebrated goals by pointing to the sky for her ever since.

The growth hormone diagnosis came when Messi was 11. His father worked at a steel factory and his mother cleaned houses. The cost of treatment was beyond the family. Newell's Old Boys, Messi's club, could not fund it. River Plate passed. Barcelona's scout did not.

## Barcelona: the making of a god

Messi arrived at La Masia, Barcelona's academy, in 2000. He trained alongside players who are now forgotten. He was quiet, small, already extraordinary. By 16, he was training with the first team. At 17 years and 3 months, he made his first-team debut under Frank Rijkaard. He became the youngest player to score for Barcelona in an official match. The records started early and never stopped.

Under Pep Guardiola, between 2008 and 2012, Messi reached something that had not been seen before. The 2009 Champions League final, a 2-0 win over Manchester United in Rome, was his first European title. Then came 2010, 2011: La Liga, Copa del Rey, Champions League, FIFA Club World Cup, UEFA Super Cup. Trophies stacked up like the game had decided to give everything it had to one player at once.

In 2012, Messi scored 91 goals in a single calendar year. It was not a record that edged past a previous mark. It shattered the previous best by more than 30 goals. That year, he scored in 21 consecutive La Liga matches. The question of whether any individual could do more in a single sport was genuinely raised.

Four consecutive Ballon d'Or awards, from 2009 to 2012. Then a gap. Then a fifth in 2019, a sixth in 2021. Eight in total, each one a statement that the sport had not produced anything like this before.

At Barcelona, Messi scored 672 goals in 778 appearances. He won 10 La Liga titles. He won the Champions League four times. He won the Copa del Rey seven times. Alongside Xavi Hernandez and Andres Iniesta, he was part of the greatest club team most football people had ever watched.

## The Argentina burden

But Argentina. This is where Messi's story became something more complicated and more human than any club career could contain.

He made his senior debut for Argentina in August 2005, aged 18. He was sent off 47 seconds into that debut for a reckless high arm. He came back. The expectation was immediate and crushing: this was Maradona's successor, the next man who would carry Argentina to a World Cup.

The weight of that comparison is difficult to overstate. Maradona had won a World Cup in 1986 almost singlehandedly, scoring five goals and producing the greatest individual performance in tournament history. Argentina needed Messi to do the same, and they needed it now, and they needed it every time he put on the blue and white shirt.

He carried it. He carried it for 21 years. And for much of that time it was not enough.

The 2014 World Cup in Brazil: Messi led Argentina to the final. He scored four goals, won the Golden Ball as the tournament's best player, and watched Mario Gotze score the winner for Germany in extra time. He sat on the Maracana turf afterwards and wept.

The 2015 Copa America: final, defeated by Chile on penalties. The 2016 Copa America: final, defeated by Chile on penalties again. After that loss, Messi stood before journalists and announced his retirement from international football. Argentina cried. The country pleaded. Three months later he was back.

He was booed at the Estadio Monumental. He was held responsible for every loss, every tight win, every match that did not reach the standard Maradona had set in the imagination of a nation. The comparisons were not kind. Some were cruel.

## Qatar 2022: the wait ends

The 2022 World Cup in Qatar was different from the first minute. A different Messi, or the same Messi with a different squad around him: Lautaro Martinez, Julian Alvarez, Di Maria in one of the great final performances, the goalkeeper Emiliano Martinez making saves that should not have been possible.

Argentina lost their first match, to Saudi Arabia in one of the tournament's greatest upsets. They recovered. They won match after match. They beat Netherlands in the quarter-finals on penalties. They beat Croatia in the semi-final 3-0, Messi scoring and assisting. They met France in the final.

That final was the greatest World Cup final since 1970. Argentina led 2-0 with 10 minutes remaining. France scored twice in 90 seconds. Extra time. More goals. Penalties. Emiliano Martinez saved two French penalties. Argentina won the World Cup for the first time since Maradona in 1986.

Messi was the tournament's best player beyond argument. Seven goals, three assists. He stood with the trophy and the 36 years of waiting collapsed around him. The Maracana had given him only tears in 2014. The Lusail Stadium gave him the only thing left to win.

## The final chapter and the Inter Miami years

After Qatar, Messi left Barcelona for good: PSG first, then the move that surprised everyone, Inter Miami in Major League Soccer. At 36, he could have taken one final European paycheck. He chose something different. He chose Miami, and Beckham's club, and the chance to play with Luis Suarez and Sergio Busquets again, and the chance to build something in a country where football was still growing into what it would become.

The 2026 World Cup in North America was always going to be his last. At 38, playing in a country that had co-hosted the tournament, Messi led Argentina to the semi-finals, then the final. They lost to Spain 1-0 in extra time, Torres scoring in the 106th minute. Messi played every minute. He scored eight goals, took his World Cup total to 20 across six tournaments, and received the Silver Ball as the second-best player.

Two days after that final, he wrote the retirement note in his notebook. Six weeks later, he posted it.

His last Argentina match was 6 October 2026, at the Estadio Mas Monumental, against Benin, in front of 84,000 people who stayed to sing for an hour after the final whistle. He walked a slow lap of the ground. He waved. He went back to Miami.

## What he leaves

Eight Ballon d'Or awards. Over 900 career goals. 98 for Argentina in 192 appearances. A World Cup gold medal. A World Cup silver medal. Four Champions League titles. Ten La Liga titles. Every major individual honour the sport offers. And the memory of movement, of the way a small man from Rosario could receive the ball in traffic and emerge free on the other side by doing something that defied simple description.

He is not finished as a footballer. Inter Miami have him until 2028, and at 39 he is still leading MLS in goal contributions.

But the blue and white shirt is done. The career that began with a contract on a napkin in Barcelona and a growth hormone injection that a scout believed was worth paying for has produced the most decorated individual in football history. The boy from Rosario who needed help just to grow became the man the sport built itself around for twenty years.

"Thank you, God," he wrote at the end of his retirement note, "for making me Argentine."

Argentina have been saying thank you back, in stadiums and on streets and on billboards and in songs, for twenty years. They will not stop now.
