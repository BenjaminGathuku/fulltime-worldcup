Two days after the World Cup final, alone with a notebook, Lionel Messi wrote the words Argentina did not yet know were coming.

"After the time that has passed since the final," he wrote, "and after giving it a lot of thought, I want to let everyone know that I am retiring from the Argentina national team."

He kept the note private for six weeks. He went back to Miami. He scored four goals in a single match against CF Montreal. He led Inter Miami's charge up the Eastern Conference. And then, on a Monday morning at the end of August, he posted a photograph of that handwritten page on Instagram, and the chapter Argentina had been postponing finally closed.

"Time is running short," the post said. "Chapters come to a close, and this is one that hurts me deeply."

## The announcement that stopped Argentina

The retirement came on 31 August 2026, 43 days after Ferran Torres's 106th-minute goal had made Spain world champions at Argentina's expense. The timing was deliberate. Messi had said after the final that he needed time with his family before making any decisions. The six-week pause was that reflection made visible.

What he released was not a press conference or a media statement. It was a photograph of handwritten words, in Spanish, in a plain notebook, the way a man writes when he is writing for himself rather than for an audience.

The note said he had given everything. That great young players were coming through who deserved their chance. That he would always love the national team and the country it represented.

"I've given it my all, I have nothing left to give," Messi wrote, "and besides, there are some really great young players coming through behind me who deserve their chance."

Rodrigo De Paul, his Inter Miami and Argentina teammate, responded on social media, writing in Spanish of Messi's unconditional love for the country, his internal battles never to give up, and his leadership with every player he shared a pitch with.

## The numbers behind the impossible

Argentina arranged a farewell friendly against Benin at the Estadio Mas Monumental in Buenos Aires on 6 October 2026. The statistics that accompanied Messi into that stadium require a moment to sit with.

192 appearances for Argentina. 98 international goals. A senior career spanning 21 years from his debut in 2005 to his farewell in October 2026. Six World Cups, one winner's medal. Two Copa Americas. The Finalissima in 2022.

And the individual awards: three World Cup Golden Balls (2014, 2022, and the 2026 Silver Ball as runner-up), 20 World Cup goals across six tournaments.

He leaves two goals short of the international century, which will haunt statistical record-keepers for decades and bother precisely nobody in Argentina.

## The night at the Monumental

The Estadio Mas Monumental holds more than 84,000 people. On 6 October, every seat was taken. Benin, chosen as a friendly opponent for a night that was always going to be about the occasion rather than the result, received a respectful reception and were treated to 90 minutes of Messi doing what only Messi does: the movement, the passing, the two or three moments per half where time appeared to behave differently around him than around everyone else on the pitch.

He confirmed before the match, and again after, that Inter Miami would be where the story continued. The contract runs to 2028. He is still leading MLS in goal contributions at 39 years old. The farewell was specific: to the blue and white shirt, to the Albiceleste, to two decades of carrying a nation's football on his shoulders.

When the whistle went, Messi walked a slow lap of the Monumental. The crowd of 84,000 stayed. They sang long after he had left the pitch, the chants echoing off concrete that had held Maradona's ghost for forty years and now had a second one to keep.

## What Argentina retires with him

The easy version of Messi's Argentina career focuses on the wait: the 2014 final defeat, the Copa America final losses in 2015 and 2016, the brief 2016 retirement that lasted three months before he came back. The version where the point is the struggle.

The fuller version is richer. Messi made his debut at 18 into a country where comparison to Maradona was not a compliment but a demand. He was booed at the Monumental before he was cheered there. He carried squads through years when the system did not serve him, when the players around him were not calibrated to what he could do, when tactics were built around protecting the team rather than expressing their best player.

He changed all of that eventually, or the team changed around him, or both things happened together, which is closest to the truth. The 2021 Copa America, in the same Maracana where Argentina lost the 2014 final, was the first crack in the wall. The 2022 World Cup in Qatar, his greatest sustained performance across seven matches, was the demolition of it.

He leaves with a 2022 World Cup gold medal and a 2026 World Cup silver, and the knowledge that at 39, in what everyone understood to be his last tournament, he dragged Argentina to the final again: the escape against Egypt in the round of 16, the cross for Lautaro in the quarter-final, the eight goals across the tournament at an age when most players are finishing their last season somewhere in a lower league.

"Thank you, God, for making me Argentine," he wrote at the end of his Instagram post.

The Estadio Monumental echoed for an hour after the match. Argentina know what they had. They know it better now that they can no longer call on it.

Inter Miami have him until 2028. After that, only Messi knows what comes next. He said, when he joined the MLS club in 2023, that it would be his last. The last chapter is still being written, just in pink and black rather than blue and white.
