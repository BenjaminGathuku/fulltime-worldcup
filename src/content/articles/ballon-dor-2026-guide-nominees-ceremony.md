On the evening of 26 October 2026, the London Palladium will host something it has never hosted before: the Ballon d'Or ceremony. For the first time in the award's history, football's most prestigious individual prize will be handed out in the English capital, and the two men most likely to receive it have given the sport a genuinely compelling debate to resolve.

Harry Kane or Lamine Yamal. Goals or glory. The striker who broke every record in front of him, or the teenager who won the only thing that matters.

Here is everything you need to know.

## The ceremony: London Palladium, 26 October

The decision to move the ceremony from its traditional Paris home to London represents a significant shift for France Football, the magazine that has awarded the Ballon d'Or since 1956. The London Palladium, one of the world's most famous entertainment venues, will welcome the game's biggest names on a Monday evening that promises to be one of the most watched events in football's calendar.

The ceremony begins at 8pm local time (11pm Nairobi, 3pm ET). The winner is chosen by 100 international football journalists, with voting having closed on 28 September 2026. The result is already decided. What remains is the reveal.

## The 30-man shortlist

France Football announced 30 nominees for the 2026 award. The shortlist by club breaks down as follows.

Paris Saint-Germain lead with 10 nominees: Khvicha Kvaratskhelia, Ousmane Dembele, Achraf Hakimi, Vitinha, Joao Neves, Fabian Ruiz, Ferran Torres, Marquinhos, Nuno Mendes and Willian Pacho.

Bayern Munich have four: Harry Kane, Michael Olise, Luis Diaz and Dayot Upamecano.

Real Madrid have four: Kylian Mbappe, Jude Bellingham, Vinicius Jr and Marc Cucurella.

Arsenal have three: Declan Rice, Gabriel and William Saliba.

Barcelona have three: Lamine Yamal, Rodri and Pau Cubarsi.

The remaining six nominees are Bruno Fernandes (Manchester United), Erling Haaland (Manchester City), Lautaro Martinez (Inter Milan), Lionel Messi (Inter Miami), Sadio Mane (Al Nassr) and Julian Quinones (Al Qadsiah).

## The notable absences

The shortlist generated as much conversation for who was left out as for who made it. Raphinha, who has scored 12 La Liga goals in seven games for Barcelona this season, did not make the cut. Desire Doue, one of the most exciting young players in Europe, was absent. Pedri, Dani Olmo, Julian Alvarez and Gavi were all overlooked.

Most significantly, Cristiano Ronaldo is not on the list. Whether this reflects a shift in how voters assess his current contribution at Al Nassr, or simply the weight of competition at this particular moment in football history, his absence from a Ballon d'Or shortlist marks a significant moment. No goalkeeper made the list either, which continues a pattern that has drawn criticism from those who argue the award undervalues defensive positions.

The surprise inclusions were Sadio Mane and Julian Quinones, whose presence raised eyebrows. Mane's nomination represents a recognition of consistent quality in the Saudi Pro League. Quinones, at Al Qadsiah, is the most unexpected name on any shortlist in recent memory.

## The race: Kane and Yamal

When the shortlist landed, the betting markets settled quickly into a two-horse race. Harry Kane was the early favourite, reflecting his extraordinary statistical output across the reference period. Lamine Yamal then moved ahead as the voting deadline approached, with his World Cup winner's medal giving him an advantage in the category that has historically carried the most weight: major international honours.

As of early October, Yamal sits at approximately 10/11 with leading bookmakers. Kane is just behind at 6/5. The remaining candidates are distant: Kylian Mbappe at 22/1, Lionel Messi at 25/1, Rodri at 40/1.

Kane's case rests on numbers. Thirty-six Bundesliga goals. Six World Cup goals. The European Golden Shoe. A total return across club and country that no other player on the shortlist can match in raw scoring terms. His Bayern Munich side won the Bundesliga, the DFB-Pokal and the German Super Cup. He was, by the most straightforward measure available, the best striker in the world over the relevant period.

Yamal's case rests on history. At 19, he would become the youngest winner ever. He won La Liga with Barcelona and was named the league's Player of the Season. He played the full 120 minutes as Spain beat Argentina in the World Cup final. He finished runner-up to Dembele in last year's vote, meaning the journalists who elect the winner have already placed him at the very top of the game.

The question the 100 voters had to answer on 28 September was which of these two arguments carries more weight. Goals without a World Cup, or a World Cup without the goals.

## The other contenders

Rodri won the World Cup Golden Ball as Spain's outstanding player in the tournament. His case was damaged by an injury-disrupted club season, and his 40/1 odds reflect the sense that voters appreciate his contribution without placing him ahead of the two leaders. He is now at Barcelona, adding to a trophy cabinet that includes a Champions League title, two Premier League titles and two European Championships.

Mbappe had 42 club goals and won the World Cup Golden Boot with a remarkable return across the tournament. But Real Madrid's domestic struggles, sitting fourth in La Liga, and France's exit before the final have cost him. His 22/1 price reflects genuine quality undermined by a lack of team silverware at the highest level.

Messi, at 39, had the kind of World Cup campaign that would define most players' careers: eight goals, the Silver Ball as the second-best player in the tournament, and a farewell to the Argentina shirt that moved a nation. His 25/1 odds suggest he is expected to finish in the top five rather than win.

Kvaratskhelia won the Champions League Player of the Season award for his performances in PSG's run to the title. His stats in the knockout rounds were extraordinary. But Georgia's failure to qualify for the World Cup left him without the international stage that the Ballon d'Or's evaluation period now heavily weights.

## What to watch for on 26 October

Beyond the main award, the ceremony covers the women's Ballon d'Or, the Kopa Trophy for the best player under 21, the Yashin Trophy for the best goalkeeper, and the Club of the Year award. Each category will generate its own debate.

For the main prize, the announcement order will tell part of the story. In recent years, France Football has built tension by revealing the top five in reverse order. The moment that separates Kane from Yamal, or confirms one of the longer shots as an upset, will be one of the defining moments of the football calendar this autumn.

The London Palladium. 26 October. One winner. After a World Cup summer that produced drama, heartbreak, and Messi's farewell, the Ballon d'Or ceremony will serve as the final punctuation mark on a remarkable twelve months of football.
