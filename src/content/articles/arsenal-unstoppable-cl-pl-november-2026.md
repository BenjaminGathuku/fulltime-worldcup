Eighteen months ago, Arsenal ended a 21-year wait to become Premier League champions. The question everyone was asking: could they sustain it? Could this be a dynasty, or a one-season wonder?

Eleven games into the 2026-27 season — nine in the Premier League, four in the Champions League — the answer is looking increasingly emphatic.

Arsenal lead the Premier League by three points with eight wins from nine. They are the only English side with a perfect record in the Champions League, sitting on 10 points from four games. Mikel Arteta's side are, by any objective measure, the best team in England right now.

So — are they unstoppable?

---

## The Premier League Picture

Arsenal's Premier League title defence has been everything they would have hoped for. Eight wins and a draw in nine games, 24 points, and already three clear of Manchester City in second.

What's striking is the breadth of their dominance. This isn't a team grinding out 1-0 wins (though they can do that too). They are winning with authority across multiple systems, against different types of opponents.

Pep Guardiola's Manchester City, now under Enzo Maresca, are three behind and have already dropped six points. Xabi Alonso's Chelsea project is progressing but they're eight points adrift. Liverpool under Iraola look solid but not yet like title challengers.

The standout surprise is **Brighton in third**. Roberto De Zerbi's former club are playing the kind of fluid, expansive football that has become their trademark, and at this pace they would be genuine top-four contenders.

At the bottom, things are bleaker for some historically significant clubs. Manchester United have just seven points from nine games, sitting 17th. Crystal Palace and Nottingham Forest are in the relegation zone. Sunderland, in their first season back in the top flight, are bottom with five points.

---

## The Champions League: Arsenal's European Ambition

For all their Premier League success, Arsenal have never won the Champions League. That itch remains unscratched — but this season's European campaign has been their most compelling in years.

Four games, four wins, 10 points. Wednesday's 2-1 victory away at Slavia Prague was professional, calm, efficient. This is a team that knows how to win on the road.

The question for Arsenal is whether their squad depth can sustain a two-front campaign. This is where the last couple of seasons have exposed them — moments when injuries or fatigue in February and March have cost them dearly.

What gives cause for optimism this time:

**1. The squad is deeper.** Summer additions have given Arteta more options across the pitch. The World Cup provided a showcase, and Arsenal moved quickly.

**2. Arteta is rotating smarter.** Against Slavia Prague, he made multiple changes from the weekend lineup. The mentality of "every competition, every game" has been drilled into the dressing room.

**3. Odegaard is fit and in form.** When Martin Odegaard is at his best, Arsenal function at a different level. He has been central to their early-season dominance, and despite the summer rumour-mongering about Bayern Munich (emphatically dismissed by credible sources this week), he is going nowhere.

---

## The Threat on the Horizon

Arsenal are not unchallenged. PSG and Real Madrid are both unbeaten in the Champions League with maximum points after four games, and both would represent an enormous test in the knockout rounds.

In the Premier League, City won't go away quietly. Under Maresca they have already shown the attacking variety and tactical flexibility to hurt anyone. And Brighton — if they can sustain their form through the fixture pile-up — might be more than just a nuisance.

The January window will also matter. With Antonio Nusa strongly linked and Marcus Tavernier reportedly on their radar, Arsenal know that addressing their attacking depth in January could be the difference between a historic double and another near-miss.

---

## Arteta's Legacy

Here is the larger context. When Mikel Arteta took over in December 2019, Arsenal were 15th in the Premier League. Five and a half years later, they are defending champions with the best start to a season any Arsenal side has managed since the Invincibles of 2003-04.

The rebuild has been meticulous, patient, and brilliantly executed. The club structure under Edu and the board has been transformed. The playing style is identifiable and consistent. The culture, by all accounts, is one of the best in world football.

This is not just one good season. This is a team built to compete for years.

---

## The Verdict

Unstoppable? No team in football history has been truly unstoppable. Everyone has bad days, bad runs, bad injuries.

But right now, in early November 2026, Arsenal are as close to it as anyone in the Premier League. They are defending their title with authority, progressing in Europe with purpose, and they have a manager and a squad that have made winning look like the default.

Fans of every other club have been warned.
