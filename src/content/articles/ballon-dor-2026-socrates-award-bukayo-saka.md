Bukayo Saka received the Socrates Award at Monday's Ballon d'Or ceremony in London, the prize that recognises a player's combination of sporting excellence and commitment to social causes beyond the football pitch.

## The Award and What It Recognises

The Socrates Award, introduced by France Football in 2021, is named after the Brazilian midfielder who was as well known for his political engagement and community activism as for his football. Socrates captained Brazil at the 1982 World Cup and spent much of his life advocating for workers' rights and democratic values. The award in his name seeks players who reflect that combination of sporting and social purpose.

Saka's win recognises a body of work that extends well beyond football. The Arsenal and England forward has been consistently involved in community initiatives across north-west London, where he grew up in Ealing. His public profile, rather than being deployed solely for commercial purposes, has repeatedly been used to draw attention to issues affecting young people in his community and beyond.

## Saka's Broader Profile

On the pitch, Saka is one of the Premier League's most complete wide forwards. His combination of technical quality, work rate, and ability to function in multiple attacking roles has made him indispensable for Arsenal and a central figure for the England national team. His World Cup this summer with England, who reached the final before losing to Spain, added an international dimension to a profile that had previously been defined by his excellence at club level.

Off the pitch, Saka's approach has been notably grounded for a player of his profile. He has spoken publicly about the racist abuse he received following England's Euro 2020 penalty miss, and his response to that abuse, maintaining dignity and continuing to perform at the highest level, made him a figure of considerable significance beyond football.

The Socrates Award voters, who include football personalities and humanitarian representatives, have recognised that significance in awarding him the 2026 prize.

## A Saka and Arsenal Connection

Arsenal have been one of the most prominent clubs at the 2026 Ballon d'Or ceremony, with three nominees in the men's shortlist: Declan Rice, Gabriel, and William Saliba. Rice finished 15th, Gabriel 21st, and Saliba joint 29th in the men's rankings announced during the ceremony.

Saka's Socrates Award win adds a fourth piece of recognition for the club on an evening where Arsenal's contributions to English and international football have been noted throughout the proceedings. The Socrates Award may not carry the same profile as the Ballon d'Or itself, but it recognises something that the main prize does not: the player's impact as a human being, not just an athlete.
