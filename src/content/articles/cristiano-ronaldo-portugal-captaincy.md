The armband changes what a player is. For some, it is a formality, something you wear because you are the most experienced player on the pitch and the manager needs to designate someone. For Cristiano Ronaldo, it was never a formality. The Portugal captaincy was a weight he chose to carry, and he carried it in ways that were sometimes magnificent, sometimes excessive, and always unmistakable.

## How He Became Captain

Ronaldo inherited the Portugal captaincy in the late 2000s as the generation of Figo and Rui Costa moved out of the picture. By then he was already the best player in the country and one of the best in the world. The captaincy was the natural next step, and he took it with the same intensity he brought to everything else.

The question for any great player who becomes captain is whether the leadership makes the team better or whether it makes the team dependent. For Portugal, over 15 years, the answer was consistently both things at once.

## What His Captaincy Looked Like

Ronaldo as captain was visible in every game. This is worth noting because many captains lead quietly, through example, through the way they train and the standards they set without speaking. Ronaldo was not that kind of captain.

He was vocal, demonstrative, sometimes infuriating to watch from the outside. He gestured to teammates, pointed where he wanted the ball, reacted visibly to missed chances. The emotions ran close to the surface. The expectations he set, for himself and for everyone around him, were higher than anyone else was setting.

His teammates over the years have spoken about what it was like to play in his team. The word that comes up most often is intensity. The belief that every game could be won, that every deficit could be overturned, that the impossible was simply a category that Ronaldo had not yet engaged with.

## Euro 2016: Captaincy Under Pressure

The defining moment of Ronaldo's captaincy came not when he was playing but when he was not. In the final of Euro 2016 against France in Paris, he went off injured in the first half. Portugal were level at that point, but losing their captain and best player against the host nation looked, in the moment, like the end.

It was not. Ronaldo spent the second half and extra time on the touchline in a knee brace, directing players, pointing, shouting, tears drying on his face. He was more animated as a spectator than many captains are as players. When Eder scored in extra time and Portugal won 1-0, Ronaldo was on the pitch within seconds, part of the celebration, the captain who had not played the final but had not for a moment stopped captaining.

That image, of a player leading from outside the pitch because the pitch would not let him in, is what made the Euro 2016 triumph feel distinctly his, even though others scored the goals and kept the clean sheets.

## The Difficult Parts

The captaincy also carried difficult elements that became more pronounced as Ronaldo aged. The tendency to centralise the team's offensive game around himself created friction at tournaments. Managers sometimes struggled to build flexible tactical structures because any system had to accommodate the captain's expectation of being the focal point.

At the 2026 World Cup, the tension between Ronaldo's standing as captain and the realistic assessment of what a 41-year-old could contribute at a major tournament was at the heart of the criticism directed at him. The new manager, Jorge Jesus, eventually had the confrontation that all previous managers had either avoided or managed carefully. Ronaldo left the squad. The retirement announcement followed.

Whether the ending was entirely on his terms or partly forced by circumstances is a question that will be discussed for a while. What is not in question is that he held the armband for 15 years, and during those 15 years Portugal won things they had never won before.

## What He Leaves for the Next Captain

The next Portugal captain inherits a programme that expects to win. That expectation was not there in 2008 when Ronaldo first took the armband. It was built by him, over 15 years of refusing to accept that Portugal was a country that celebrated near-misses and third places.

Whoever takes the armband next, whether it is Bruno Fernandes or Ruben Dias or someone emerging from the next generation, they will carry it into a situation that Ronaldo created. The major tournament wins, the ranked-player depth in the squad, the fact that Portugal goes into every Euros and World Cup as a genuine contender rather than a hopeful outsider.

That is the captaincy's legacy, beyond the goals and the records. He changed what Portugal expected of themselves. That change will outlast him, and it is probably the most significant thing the armband produced across those 15 years.
