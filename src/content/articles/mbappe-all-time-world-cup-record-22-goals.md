Miroslav Klose's 16 stood for twelve years. Lionel Messi broke it at this very tournament, in June 2026, in the group stage, and became the sport's most prolific World Cup scorer at age 39.

Kylian Mbappé broke Messi's mark six weeks later. He is 27.

The speed with which this record is moving hands tells you something important: the men who hold it keep getting younger, and the man who holds it now may have barely started.

## How he got to 22

Mbappé's World Cup career began as a teenager in 2018 in Russia, where he arrived as a largely unknown 19-year-old and left as a revelation: four goals, France's title, and the kind of impact that makes entire nations simultaneously terrified and grateful. He became only the second teenager to score in a World Cup final, after Pelé in 1958.

2022 in Qatar pushed the story further. A hat-trick in the final. The greatest individual performance in a losing final the game has arguably seen, with France falling to Argentina on penalties after Mbappé's three goals hauled the deficit back from 2–0. He left Qatar with eight World Cup goals at 24 years old, the same total Ronaldo Nazário finished his career on.

Then 2026, and the demolition. Ten goals from eight matches: a brace against Sweden, the penalty against Paraguay, a stunning answer to his own missed spot-kick against Morocco, and two more against England in Miami that moved him past Messi and into history. Golden Boot at consecutive tournaments. A record that now reads 22, in capital letters, from 26 appearances.

## What 27 means

Klose's 16 arrived at the end of four World Cups, the last of which he played at 36. Messi's 20 came at the end of six tournaments, the last played at 39. Mbappé has 22 at the end of his third.

The arithmetic gets uncomfortable for everyone else quickly. He is, barring injury or retirement, the favourite to play the 2030 World Cup in Spain, Portugal and Morocco. Realistic target: four to six more goals before he turns 32. Total: somewhere between 26 and 28. Then, potentially, 2034. The record Klose spent sixteen years building, that Messi spent a decade chasing, may end up being simply a number Mbappé passed on the way somewhere else.

The obvious challenge is the one nobody can answer: nobody stays healthy through five World Cups, and nobody who has that many records to break is playing for records. In 2022, Mbappé needed extra time and penalties just to get a chance at lifting the trophy -- and lost. In 2026, Spain were a level above France in the semi-final, and the trophy remained out of reach. The record now belongs to him. The one prize that defines World Cup legacy, the one he has chased both tournaments, still doesn't.

## The argument about greatness

The debate Mbappé's record opens is one the sport has no clean answer to: does the scoring record matter as much as the winner's medal? Messi's 20 goals came alongside two finals -- one lost, one won. Mbappé's 22 came alongside a 2018 title and two semi-finals. Klose's 16 included a 2014 winner's medal that Mbappé's total has already surpassed numerically.

The conventional answer from football is that trophies define greatness. But the scoring record carries its own gravity -- it is the thing that will appear on every future record-holder's Wikipedia page as the benchmark they broke, and Mbappé's name is now permanently attached to a number that used to belong to Messi.

He has at least two World Cups still theoretically available. The conversation about where his career record ends has barely begun. At 27, holding the all-time mark at his third tournament, Kylian Mbappé is not the inheritor of an era. He is the era.
