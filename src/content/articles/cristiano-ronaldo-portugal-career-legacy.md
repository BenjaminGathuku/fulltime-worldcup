There is a version of the Cristiano Ronaldo story that gets told through individual brilliance. The goals, the records, the impossible athleticism maintained into his forties. That version is accurate, but it is incomplete. The fuller version, the one that emerges from 23 years of wearing the Portugal shirt, is about something more complicated and more compelling: a player who carried a country's entire international ambition on his shoulders, and delivered.

Not always. Not at every tournament. Sometimes badly, sometimes brilliantly, never without significance. When he stepped away from the Portugal shirt this week, the No. 7 was the most iconic item of clothing in the history of Portuguese sport.

## How It Began

Ronaldo made his Portugal debut in 2003, three months before Sir Alex Ferguson took him to Manchester United. He was 18, playing for Sporting Lisbon, the best young player in the country by some distance. The national team was already strong. Luis Figo, Rui Costa, Fernando Couto. Ronaldo came into a team with established stars and established habits, and he was not yet the dominant force he would become.

By Euro 2004, one year later, he had already announced himself. Portugal hosted the tournament and reached the final, losing to Greece in one of the biggest upsets in European Championship history. Ronaldo scored, created, and cried when it was over. He was 19. He had already told the world what was coming.

## The Figo to Ronaldo Transition

Luis Figo had been Portugal's defining player through the late 1990s and early 2000s. His number was different, his style was different, but his importance to the national team was similar in kind if not in scale to what Ronaldo would become. When Figo stepped back from international football, there was a question of whether Portugal could fill the void.

Ronaldo answered it immediately. He did not just fill the void left by Figo. He expanded it, turned it into something larger and more demanding, and then met those demands himself. The number 7 shirt, which Figo had worn before him, became something it had never been before. A statement. An identity. A promise.

## Euro 2016: The One That Mattered

Portugal went into Euro 2016 in France without anyone's strong expectation of winning it. They had a great player. The team around him was solid. But France were the hosts and the favourites, and Germany and Spain had been winning tournaments for years.

Portugal won it anyway.

Ronaldo's tournament was complicated. He scored twice in the group stage and drew criticism for misfires and missed chances. By the semi-final he had imposed himself more decisively, and against Wales in the semi-final he scored twice, the second a header of pure predatory instinct, to put Portugal in the final.

Then injury took him off in the final in Paris. France had the better of it after he left. Portugal somehow did not concede. And then Eder, a substitute, scored in extra time, and Portugal were European champions for the first time in their history.

The image of Ronaldo on the touchline, heavily strapped, barking instructions, tears drying on his face, is one of the defining images of his international career. The trophy belongs to everyone who played. The energy that pushed them through the tournament, the belief that the whole thing was possible, the refusal to accept that a small country with a good team could not win a major tournament, that came from him.

Euro 2016 was Portugal's only major tournament win. Ronaldo's only major international trophy. It is enough.

## The Nations League Years

After Euro 2016, Portugal won the inaugural UEFA Nations League in 2019 and added a second Nations League title during Ronaldo's later international years. The Nations League carries less prestige than the World Cup or Euros, but winning trophies is not nothing, and Portugal winning two of them reflects a team that had moved from occasionally competitive to consistently winning.

Ronaldo was central to both. At 34 and later in his career, his contributions shifted from pure explosive attacking play to leadership, set pieces, penalty taking, and the ability to produce a decisive moment from almost nothing. The goals kept coming. His physical decline was real but managed carefully. He knew what his body could and could not do, and he built his game around it.

## The World Cup That Wasn't

The absence in Ronaldo's record that matters most is the World Cup. He appeared at six of them. He scored at all of them. Portugal never won one.

The 2026 tournament in North America was his last. It was a difficult one, with Portugal exiting before the final and significant criticism directed at Ronaldo for starting up front as the gap between his reputation and his current physical contribution widened. The criticism was not entirely fair. It was also not entirely wrong.

He wanted to go further. He always wanted to go further. The World Cup was the one thing he could not make happen by force of will alone.

## The Record That Cannot Be Touched

One hundred and forty-six goals in 234 appearances. These are records that belong to Ronaldo alone in the men's international game. They were set over 23 years, against every standard of opposition, in qualification, in group stages, in knockouts, in tournaments that mattered and in games that felt like the end of the world.

No one will catch them quickly. The gap between Ronaldo and the next-highest scorer in men's international football is too large, the appearances record too far ahead of the second-placed player. His records will stand for the generation that follows him, possibly longer.

## What He Leaves Behind

He leaves behind a Portugal team that expects to be competitive at every tournament. That expectation did not exist before him. It was built by him, game by game, goal by goal, over two decades.

He leaves behind a generation of young Portuguese players, Bruno Fernandes, Ruben Dias, Bernardo Silva and others, who grew up watching him play and internalized something of his refusal to accept that Portugal was a small country with modest ambitions.

He leaves behind 146 goals and 234 caps and one European Championship trophy and two Nations League titles.

He leaves behind the number 7 shirt, waiting for whoever is brave enough to wear it next.
