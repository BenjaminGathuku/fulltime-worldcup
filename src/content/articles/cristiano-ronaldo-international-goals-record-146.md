One hundred and forty-six. That is the number of times Cristiano Ronaldo scored for Portugal across 23 years and 234 appearances. It is the highest total in the history of men's international football, a record he built over two decades and left so far ahead of the next-placed player that it may stand for a generation.

The record did not arrive suddenly. It was accumulated incrementally, goal by goal, across qualification campaigns and group stages and knockout matches and friendlies, against opposition ranging from San Marino to France in a European Championship final. The total was always rising. The circumstances varied. The scorer did not.

## How the Record Was Built

Ronaldo's goal-scoring for Portugal followed the same pattern that defined his club career: penalties, free-kicks, headed goals, left-foot finishes from outside the box, right-foot tap-ins from two yards, goals in every conceivable situation and from every conceivable position.

He was lethal from the penalty spot, reliable from set pieces, and deadly in and around the six-yard box in a way that combined his physical power with a striker's reading of where the ball was going to arrive. Many of his goals came from situations that required the specific combination of athleticism and anticipation he had spent 20 years developing.

The range mattered because it made him impossible to fully contain. A team that packed the box against him got beaten by his free-kicks. A team that pressed him high conceded in behind. A team that matched him physically found that his technical quality was sharper than theirs. There was no single solution to Cristiano Ronaldo.

## The Records He Broke Along the Way

Before Ronaldo, the men's international goals record was held by Ali Daei of Iran, who scored 109 goals in 149 appearances. Daei's record had stood for years as the benchmark. Ronaldo passed it in 2021, against Ireland in a World Cup qualifier, equalising in the 89th minute and then scoring the winner in the 96th to claim both the record and the three points in a single match.

The manner of breaking the record was characteristic. The two goals came late, when Portugal needed them, against a team that had almost held them. The record-breaking moment was not a soft friendly or a ceremonial cap. It was two goals in added time in a competitive qualifier. It was vintage Ronaldo.

He then kept going, extending the record from 109 to 146, adding another 37 international goals after the moment most players would have been satisfied to stop.

## Qualification Goals: The Overlooked Foundation

The majority of Ronaldo's goals came not in major tournaments but in the qualification campaigns that preceded them. World Cup qualifying, European Championship qualifying, the grinding runs of home and away fixtures against smaller nations and competitive rivals that determine whether a team gets to a major tournament at all.

This matters because qualification goals are where the record is actually built. The hat-tricks against Luxembourg and Andorra, the penalties against Hungary, the free-kicks against Sweden in a playoff when Portugal needed to win to qualify, all of these contributed to the total just as much as the goals at the World Cup or the Euros.

Ronaldo was as focused in qualification as in finals. The switch that told him some games were less important than others was one he never installed. Every cap, every game, every goal in a Portugal shirt carried the same weight to him.

## The Penalty Record Within the Record

A significant proportion of Ronaldo's international goals came from the penalty spot. He was Portugal's designated penalty taker across almost his entire international career, and his conversion rate was high throughout.

His ability to score penalties consistently across 20 years, in high-pressure moments at major tournaments, is part of the story of how 146 becomes possible. Penalty goals count the same as any other goals. The discipline and reliability he brought to them, in qualifying games and in European Championship finals, turned what might have been 100 or 110 goals into 146.

## Who Comes Next

The question of when and whether Ronaldo's record will be broken does not have a clear answer. The gap between 146 and second place is large enough that the next serious contender would need to maintain an international career of similar length and similar productivity to have any chance.

There are players in the current generation who score regularly for their countries. None of them are on a trajectory that makes 146 look realistic in the medium term. The record will be there, in the books, waiting for whoever is eventually good enough and durable enough to chase it.

That could be a decade away. It could be two. It could be longer.

Ronaldo spent 23 years building it. The wall he left behind may take just as long to knock down.
