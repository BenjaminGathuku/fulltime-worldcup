Michael Carrick's position as Manchester United manager has been the defining domestic football story in England for the past two months. The club sit 16th in the Premier League, without a win in eight games across all competitions, and the pressure has been building from every direction. Yet he is still there.

## How Carrick Got the Job

The background is worth revisiting. Carrick was appointed as Manchester United interim head coach in January 2026 following the departure of Ruben Amorim. It was a familiar type of appointment for United in recent years, a bridge figure brought in to stabilise the club while a longer-term plan was formulated. What was different this time was the decision to then confirm Carrick permanently as head coach rather than use the interim period to recruit from outside.

The confirmation came with the backing of the board and the club's ownership structure. There was optimism about Carrick's ability to implement a coherent playing philosophy built on United's recently assembled squad. The summer transfer window added players who were expected to give him more options in midfield.

Five games into the new season, that optimism had largely evaporated.

## A Run That Has United at Rock Bottom

Seven points from eight Premier League games. 16th in the table. Exit from the Carabao Cup. No win in eight consecutive matches across all competitions. The numbers make uncomfortable reading for a club that considers itself among the Premier League's traditional elite.

The low point, by most assessments, was the 3-0 home defeat to Bournemouth, a result that prompted the most serious speculation yet about Carrick's future. Gary Neville, who is rarely one to call for a manager's head, admitted he had been "absolutely certain" the Bournemouth game would be Carrick's last. After the decision to keep him, Neville said: "I'm surprised but I'm happy," adding that he hoped Carrick could get more from his players.

## Romano's Report and the Board's Position

Fabrizio Romano's assessment, reported in late September, was that United had not contacted any other coaches and were not in talks with any manager or representative. His read was that the club expected the situation to stay quiet for weeks, possibly months.

The board's position, as described by journalist Pete O'Rourke to Football Insider, was that United could not afford the upheaval of another managerial change. The club had been through multiple managers in recent years, and the financial and institutional cost of that instability was something the ownership wanted to stop. Part of that calculation was the literal financial cost: sacking Carrick and his coaching staff would reportedly cost United approximately £8.8 million.

Romano confirmed the club was maintaining confidence in Carrick publicly, though acknowledging that "everyone is unhappy" with results internally.

## The Argument for Keeping Him

The case for patience is not entirely without substance. Three of United's summer signings are still building chemistry together in midfield. The squad was substantially rebuilt and cohesion takes time to develop across an entire team. The club have been through enough managerial change in recent years to know that the disruption of another change is not automatically better than the disruption of continued poor form.

The argument against is simpler: seventh months into the job, 16th in the table, no wins in eight.

## What Comes Next

Carrick was set to lead United at Stamford Bridge against Chelsea following the Bournemouth defeat. If results do not improve significantly over the next run of fixtures, the board's public support will face a sterner test than the one they have already weathered.

The £8.8m figure will continue to feature in every conversation about his future. It is a significant sum, but it is not a figure that makes change impossible. It is, however, a figure that makes change less automatic than it might otherwise be for a club in United's current standing.

United are where they are. The question that follows them into every fixture is whether the people responsible for fixing it will make a different decision before the season becomes irretrievable.
